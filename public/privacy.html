Privacy policy

Transformation Solution Services, LLC ("TSS"), an Oregon limited liability company. Effective November 13, 2026.

1. What this policy covers

The personal information TSS decides about for itself: people at a customer organization who deal with TSS (signers, named contacts, billing contacts, and staff who write to support), people who ask TSS about its products, and visitors to TSS's web pages.

2. What it does not cover

The records an organization keeps in TSS's apps, including Ledger ("customer data"). The organization decides about customer data, and TSS is its service provider, acting only on the organization's instructions and under its contract with TSS. TSS does not sell customer data, does not use it for its own purposes, and trains no model on it.

3. QuickBooks data in Ledger

When a Ledger manager connects the organization's QuickBooks Online company, Ledger reads its accounts, classes, budgets and spending transactions, and this year's profit and loss totals, to show each grant's budget, spending and balance. Ledger only reads: it never creates, changes or deletes anything in QuickBooks. The QuickBooks sign-in tokens are encrypted before they are stored, are never shown or exported, and are revoked and deleted when the organization disconnects. What Ledger read stays the organization's customer data under section 2. Intuit's own privacy statement covers what Intuit does.

4. Google data

Each person may connect their own Google account (Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Drive) in Cockpit, under Connected apps. Connecting is optional, and it connects that person's account only.

  • Gmail. Cockpit's daily brief shows the sender and subject of the person's unread inbox messages. When the person asks, Cockpit's chat and the person's own agents search and read their mail to answer. An email goes out from their Gmail only after the person approves that email. Gatsby saves a donation request as a draft in the person's Gmail; the person sends it.
  • Google Calendar. The daily brief shows the person's events for the next seven days. When the person asks, the chat and their agents read events, and add or change an event.
  • Google Drive. Library imports the files the person picks into a Library collection.

Google sign-in tokens are held, encrypted, by the connection service TSS uses (Composio), which makes each call to Google for the person. TSS's apps never see the tokens. When the person asks about a message, event or file, its text is sent to TSS's AI provider (Anthropic) to write the answer, under terms that do not allow the provider to train models on it. The brief's list of messages and events is kept in memory for two minutes. What the person asks about stays in that person's own chat history, which nobody else can see. A file imported from Drive becomes material in the Library collection it was imported to.

TSS uses Google data only to provide the features the person sees and asks for. TSS does not sell it, does not use it for advertising, does not use it to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models, and does not transfer it except as this section says, for security, or when the law requires. No person at TSS reads it unless the person asks TSS to, for security, or when the law requires.

TSS's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

A person disconnects in Cockpit under Connected apps, which deletes the connection and its tokens, or at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

5. What TSS collects

  • You give it: name, organization, role, email, phone, billing details, and what you write to TSS.
  • From the web pages: the address your browser connects from, browser type, pages asked for and the time, in the server's logs. TSS sets only the cookies needed to keep you signed in. TSS uses no analytics or advertising trackers.
  • About the service: technical facts about how the apps run (health checks, error ids, counts). These hold no customer data.

6. How TSS uses it

To provide and support the service, bill for it, answer questions, tell customers about changes and security notices, keep its systems secure, keep the records the law requires, and enforce its contracts.

7. Who TSS shares it with

Service providers that work for TSS (hosting, email, accounting) under contracts that limit their use; professional advisers under a duty of confidentiality; authorities when the law requires; and a buyer or successor of TSS's business, under this policy. TSS does not sell personal information, does not share it for targeted advertising, and trains no model on it.

8. How long TSS keeps it

Contracts, invoices and support records for the customer relationship and seven years after. Server logs for 90 days. Inquiries that lead nowhere for two years.

9. Security

Access is limited to the people who need it. Connections are encrypted in transit. QuickBooks sign-in tokens are encrypted at rest. Each organization's records are kept apart from every other organization's.

10. Children

TSS's apps are for organizations' staff and are not meant for children under 13.

11. Your rights

You may ask TSS to confirm whether it holds your personal data and get a copy, to correct it, or to delete it unless the law requires TSS to keep it. Write to TSS at the address below. TSS answers within 45 days. For customer data, TSS refers you to the organization that holds it. Oregon residents may also contact the Oregon Department of Justice.

12. Changes

TSS posts changes here with a new effective date and tells customer contacts about material ones before they take effect.
